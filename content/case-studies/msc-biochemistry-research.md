## Problem Statement

Plants run thousands of metabolic pathways capable of producing high-value
compounds — drug precursors, caffeine, and other bioactive molecules with real
socioeconomic impact. Within legumes specifically, one such pathway produces
isoflavonoids — antioxidant compounds with potential cancer-reducing properties —
but production through that pathway wasn't well understood or optimized.

## Rationale

My hypothesis was that we could enrich production of these high-value compounds by
synthetically optimizing specific steps in the pathway. Testing that required
first characterizing how variants of one key protein scaled the production of the
desired precursors.

## Approach

- Characterized how different variants of the target protein affected the scale
  of precursor production
- Developed assays to identify both the mechanism and the outcome of each variant
- Worked through significant experimental troubleshooting for most of the
  program: inconsistent gene expression in plants, unclear protein synthesis
  results, and physical lab constraints (multiple lab moves and shutdowns) that
  repeatedly interrupted the work — without losing the thread of the research

## Outcome

- Established that a specific protein (**CHIL**) is consistently required to
  enrich production through this pathway
- Found that different variants specialize differently: some raise output
  broadly across all compounds through a looser structural mechanism, while
  others are more targeted — trading overall yield for efficiency toward one
  specific branch of the pathway
- The result was accepted for **peer-reviewed publication**
