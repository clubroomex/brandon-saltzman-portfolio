## Problem Statement

When a plant is under threat — from a pest or a hostile physical environment — it
triggers an immune response similar to a human immune response, coordinated at the
cellular level through large protein hubs that assemble quickly once they detect a
threat signal. Since fleeing isn't an option for a plant, understanding how that
coordination actually works mattered for understanding plant defense more broadly.

## Rationale

Understanding which members of a key protein family drive that coordinated
response mattered for explaining how small genetic differences could scale into
large differences in a plant's ability to defend itself — the specific question my
research focused on.

## Approach

- Focused the research on the **PUB protein family**'s role in coordinating the
  plant's response to drought and pathogens
- Used computational models to identify the full set of PUB variants and select
  the most interesting candidates for comparison, based on their protein
  sequences
- Contributed findings into a larger team research effort spanning multiple
  researchers

## Outcome

- Contributed to a **published paper** characterizing how small changes in the
  genetic code of these proteins can lead to sizable differences in
  immunological response
