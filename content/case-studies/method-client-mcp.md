## Problem Statement

Method had already built strong internal AI/MCP capability for its own
Professional Services team — but Method's clients had no equivalent way to give
their own AI tools and agents stable, structured access to their account data
inside Method. Clients who wanted to build AI workflows on top of their CRM data
had no supported path to do it.

## Rationale

The internal MCP capability already existed; the bigger opportunity was exposing
that same capability to clients as a first-class product feature rather than
keeping it an internal Professional Services tool. A stable, well-scoped MCP
connection would let clients plug their own AI agents directly into the software
they already used, without waiting on custom integration work. It wasn't on the
product roadmap, so the first job was making the case for it.

## Approach

- Designed the framework for a client-facing MCP connection into Method's
  software, giving external AI agents and tools stable, structured access to a
  client's own account data
- Deliberately scoped the initial version narrow and well-bounded rather than
  trying to expose everything at once, to get something real in front of
  clients quickly
- Championed the feature internally — building the case for product leadership
  to treat this as a supported product capability rather than a one-off
  integration
- Shipped it to an initial set of clients to validate the approach before a
  broader rollout

## Outcome

- **Live with 3 clients**, giving them stable MCP access into their own Method
  data today
- Took the feature from an internal-only capability to a shipped,
  client-facing product feature — the kind of cross-functional push that
  requires making the case without formal product authority
- Positions Method to offer this more broadly as adoption and confidence grow
